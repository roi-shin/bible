# Revelation 10 (English)

## Text

**1** Then I saw another powerful angel descending from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow above his head; his face was like the sun, and his legs were like pillars of fire. 

**2** He held in his hand a little scroll that was open, and he put his right foot on the sea and his left on the land. 

**3** Then he shouted in a loud voice like a lion roaring, and when he shouted, the seven thunders sounded their voices. 

**4** When the seven thunders spoke, I was preparing to write, but just then I heard a voice from heaven say, “Seal up what the seven thunders spoke and do not write it down.” 

**5** Then the angel I saw standing on the sea and on the land raised his right hand to heaven 

**6** and swore by the one who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, and the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it, “There will be no more delay! 

**7** But in the days when the seventh angel is about to blow his trumpet, the mystery of God is completed, just as he has proclaimed to his servants the prophets.” 

**8** Then the voice I had heard from heaven began to speak to me again, “Go and take the open scroll in the hand of the angel who is standing on the sea and on the land.” 

**9** So I went to the angel and asked him to give me the little scroll. He said to me, “Take the scroll and eat it. It will make your stomach bitter, but it will be as sweet as honey in your mouth.” 

**10** So I took the little scroll from the angel’s hand and ate it, and it did taste as sweet as honey in my mouth, but when I had eaten it, my stomach became bitter. 

**11** Then they told me: “You must prophesy again about many peoples, nations, languages, and kings.” 

## Notes

### Verse 1 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “then” to indicate the implied sequence of events within the vision.

### Verse 1 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  Or “clothed.”

### Verse 1 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  Or “like fiery pillars,” translating πυρός (puros) as an attributive genitive.

### Verse 2 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Grk “and having.” Because of the length and complexity of the Greek sentence, a new sentence was started here in the translation by supplying the pronoun “he.”

### Verse 4 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  The words “just then” are not in the Greek text, but are implied.

### Verse 5 - Note 1

### Verse 6 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  On this phrase see BDAG 1092 s.v. χρόνος.

### Verse 7 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Grk “But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel.”

### Verse 7 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  The aorist ἐτελέσθη (etelesqh) has been translated as a proleptic (futuristic) aorist (ExSyn 564 cites this verse as an example).

### Verse 7 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  The time of the action described by the aorist εὐηγγέλισεν (euhngelisen) seems to be past with respect to the aorist passive ἐτελέσθη (etelesqh). This does not require that the prophets in view here be OT prophets. They may actually refer to the martyrs in the church (so G. B. Caird, Revelation [HNTC], 129).

### Verse 7 - Note 4
[翻訳注]  See the note on the word “servants” in 1:1.

### Verse 8 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “then” to indicate the implied sequence of events within the vision.

### Verse 8 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  The participle λαλοῦσαν (lalousan) has been translated as “began to speak.” The use of πάλιν (palin) indicates an ingressive idea.

### Verse 8 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  Grk “with me.” The translation “with me” implies that John was engaged in a dialogue with the one speaking to him (e.g., Jesus or an angel) when in reality it was a one-sided conversation, with John doing all the listening. For this reason, μετ᾿ ἐμοῦ (met’ emou, “with me”) was translated as “to me.”

### Verse 8 - Note 4
[翻訳注]  Grk “again, saying.” The participle λέγουσαν (legousan) is redundant in contemporary English and has not been translated.

### Verse 8 - Note 5
[翻訳注]  The perfect passive participle ἠνεῳγμένον (hnewgmenon) is in second attributive position and has been translated as an attributive adjective.

### Verse 9 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “so” to indicate the implied result of the instructions given by the voice.

### Verse 9 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has not been translated because of differences between Greek and English style.

### Verse 9 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  The words “the scroll” are not in the Greek text, but are implied. Direct objects were frequently omitted in Greek when clear from the context.

### Verse 9 - Note 4
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has not been translated because of differences between Greek and English style.

### Verse 10 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “so” to indicate the implied result of the instructions given by the angel.

### Verse 10 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  Grk “it was.” The idea of taste is implied.

### Verse 10 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “but” to indicate the contrast present in this context.

### Verse 11 - Note 1
[翻訳注]  Here καί (kai) has been translated as “then” to indicate the implied sequence of events within the vision.

### Verse 11 - Note 2
[翻訳注]  The referent of “they” is not clear in the Greek text.

### Verse 11 - Note 3
[翻訳注]  Grk “and nations,” but καί (kai) has not been translated here or before the next item since English normally uses a coordinating conjunction only between the last two elements in a series of three or more.
