エステル記 4Esther 4

1
モルデカイは行われたすべてのことを知ると、衣服を引き裂き、粗布をまとって灰をかぶった。そして町の中に出て行き、大声で激しく泣き叫んだ。
Now when Mordecai became aware of all that had been done, he tore his garments and put on sackcloth and ashes. He went out into the city, crying out in a loud and bitter voice.

But he went no farther than the king’s gate, for no one was permitted to enter the king’s gate clothed in sackcloth.
3
王の命令と法律が伝えられたすべての州で、ユダヤ人の間に大きな嘆きが起こり、断食と号泣と悲嘆が広がった。多くの者が粗布をまとい、灰の上に身を横たえた。
Throughout each and every province where the king’s edict and law were announced there was considerable mourning among the Jews, along with fasting, weeping, and sorrow. Sackcloth and ashes were characteristic of many.

エステルの侍女たちと宦官たちが来てモルデカイの様子を伝えると、王妃は深い苦痛を覚えた。彼女はモルデカイに粗布を脱がせて着せるための衣服を送ったが、彼は受け取ろうとしなかった。
When Esther’s female attendants and her eunuchs came and informed her about Mordecai’s behavior, the queen was overcome with anguish. Although she sent garments for Mordecai to put on so that he could remove his sackcloth, he would not accept them.

So Esther called for Hathach, one of the king’s eunuchs who had been placed at her service, and instructed him to find out the cause and reason for Mordecai’s behavior.

So Hathach went to Mordecai at the plaza of the city in front of the king’s gate.

Then Mordecai related to him everything that had happened to him, even the specific amount of money that Haman had offered to pay to the king’s treasuries for the Jews to be destroyed.

He also gave him a written copy of the law that had been disseminated in Susa for their destruction so that he could show it to Esther and talk to her about it. He also gave instructions that she should go to the king to implore him and petition him on behalf of her people.

“All the servants of the king and the people of the king’s provinces know that there is only one law applicable to any man or woman who comes uninvited to the king in the inner court—that person will be put to death, unless the king extends to him the gold scepter, permitting him to be spared. Now I have not been invited to come to the king for some 30 days.”

he said to take back this answer to Esther: “Don’t imagine that because you are part of the king’s household you will be the one Jew who will escape.

“Go, assemble all the Jews who are found in Susa, and fast on my behalf. Don’t eat and don’t drink for three days, night or day. My female attendants and I will also fast in the same way. Afterward I will go to the king, even though it violates the law. If I perish, I perish.”

NET Notes (翻訳者注)

1:1
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「モルデカイ」。文体上の理由から代名詞「彼」が翻訳で用いられている。現代英語の文体において、ここで固有名詞を繰り返すことは冗長となるためである。
[翻訳注] Heb “Mordecai.” The pronoun (“he”) was used in the translation for stylistic reasons. A repetition of the proper name here is redundant in terms of contemporary English style.
1:2
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「大いなる」。
[翻訳注] Heb “great.”
3:1
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「届いた」（NAB、NLTも同様）。KJV、NASB、NIVは「来た」、TEVは「王の布告が知らされたところではどこでも」としている。
[翻訳注] Heb “reached” (so NAB, NLT); KJV, NASB, NIV “came”; TEV “wherever the king’s proclamation was made known.”
3:2
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「大いなる」（KJV、NIV、NRSV、NLTも同様）。NABは「ユダヤ人は深い哀悼に沈んだ」としている。
[翻訳注] Heb “great” (so KJV, NIV, NRSV, NLT); NAB “the Jews went into deep mourning.”
3:3
[解説] ここでは祈りについて明示されていないが、この壊滅的な知らせに対する反応として、神に助けを求める祈りが含まれていなかったとは考えにくい。エステル記の他の箇所と同様に、著者は宗教的な行為を意図的に前面に出さないようにしていると見られる。
[解説] Although prayer is not specifically mentioned here, it is highly unlikely that appeals to God for help were not a part of this reaction to devastating news. As elsewhere in the book of Esther, the writer seems deliberately to keep religious actions in the background.
3:4
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「多くの者に敷かれた」。KJV、NIVは「多くの者が粗布と灰の中に横たわった」としている。
[翻訳注] Heb “were spread to many”; KJV, NIV “many (+ people NLT) lay in sackcloth and ashes.”

[翻訳注] The words “about Mordecai’s behavior” are not in the Hebrew text, but are supplied in translation for the sake of clarity. Cf. NIV, NLT “about Mordecai”; TEV, CEV “what Mordecai was doing.”

[翻訳注] Heb “stand”; KJV, NASB, NIV, NLT “arise.”
14:3
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「場所」（KJV、NIV、NLTも同様）。NRSVは「別の方角から」としている。これは神からの助けを間接的に指していると考えられる。D・J・A・クラインズは異論を唱え、エステルによる救出と神による救出を対比させることは不適切であるとする（Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther [NCBC], 302）。しかし、ユダヤ人高官による救済やユダヤ人の武装蜂起を指しているとするクラインズの説よりも、この婉曲な表現を、神の摂理による働きをあえて前面に出さないという本書の文学的手法の一環とみなす見解のほうが妥当である。
[翻訳注] Heb “place” (so KJV, NIV, NLT); NRSV “from another quarter.” This is probably an oblique reference to help coming from God. D. J. A. Clines disagrees; in his view a contrast between deliverance by Esther and deliverance by God is inappropriate (Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther [NCBC], 302). But Clines’ suggestion that perhaps the reference is to deliverance by Jewish officials or by armed Jewish revolt is less attractive than seeing this veiled reference as part of the literary strategy of the book, which deliberately keeps God’s providential dealings entirely in the background.
14:4
[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「そして〜であるか誰が知ろうか」（NASBも同様）。この問いは希望を示すものであり、思い込みとは無縁のものである。ヨナ書3章9節を参照。
[翻訳注] Heb “And who knows whether” (so NASB). The question is one of hope, but free of presumption. Cf. Jonah 3:9.

[翻訳注] ヘブライ語原典は「王国に至った」。NRSVは「王の尊厳へ」、NIVは「王の地位へ」、NLTは「宮殿に引き上げられた」としている。
[翻訳注] Heb “have come to the kingdom”; NRSV “to royal dignity”; NIV “to royal position”; NLT “have been elevated to the palace.”

[翻訳注] Heb “I and my female attendants.” The translation reverses the order for stylistic reasons.

[翻訳注] Heb “which is not according to the law” (so KJV, NASB); NAB “contrary to the law.”
