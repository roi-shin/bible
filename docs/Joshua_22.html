Butler explains the significance of the land’s “impurity”: “East Jordan is impure because it is not Yahweh’s possession. Rather it is simply ‘your possession.’ That means it is land where Yahweh does not live, land which his presence has not sanctified and purified” (Joshua [WBC], 247).

[翻訳注] この節の後半は文字通りには「そして彼は一人の男であり、彼はその罪のために死ななかった」と読める。この記述の説明には少なくとも二つの方法がある。(1) アカンだけがその罪のために死んだのではない、という意味に解釈する場合。この場合「彼だけがその罪のために死んだのではなかった」と翻訳できる。(2) もう一つの選択肢（本訳文に反映されたもの）は、וְהוּא אִישׁ אֶחָד（vÿhu’ ’ish ’ekhad、「そして彼は一人の男であった」）という語句を譲歩節として直前の文と結びつける解釈である。その場合、残りの語句（לֹא גָוַע בַּעֲוֹנוֹ, lo’ gava’ ba’avono）は修辞的疑問文（「彼はその罪のために死んだではないか」）と解釈しなければならない。末尾の文を疑問文と見なすことは、疑問詞で始まるこの節の前半の修辞的疑問文とも整合する。本訳では強調点をより明確にするため、これらの修辞的疑問文を肯定文に変換している。詳細な議論については T. C. Butler, Joshua (WBC), 240 を参照。
[翻訳注] The second half of the verse reads literally, “and he [was] one man, he did not die for his sin.” There are at least two possible ways to explain this statement: (1) One might interpret the statement to mean that Achan was not the only person who died for his sin. In this case it could be translated, “and he was not the only one to die because of his sin.” (2) Another option, the one reflected in the translation, is to take the words וְהוּא אִישׁ אֶחָד (vÿhu’ ’ish ’ekhad, “and he [was] one man”) as a concessive clause and join it with what precedes. The remaining words (לֹא גָוַע בַּעֲוֹנוֹ, lo’ gava’ ba’avono) must then be taken as a rhetorical question (“Did he not die for his sin?”). Taking the last sentence as interrogative is consistent with the first part of the verse, a rhetorical question introduced with the interrogative particle. The present translation has converted these rhetorical questions into affirmative statements to bring out more clearly the points they are emphasizing. For further discussion, see T. C. Butler, Joshua (WBC), 240.

[解説] Israel’s God is here identified with three names: (1) אֵל (’el), “El” (or “God”); (2) אֱלֹהִים (’elohim), “Elohim” (or “God”), and (3) יְהוָה (yÿhvah), “Yahweh” (or “the Lord”). The name אֵל (’el, “El”) is often compounded with titles, for example, El Elyon, “God Most High.”
