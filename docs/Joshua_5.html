ヨシュア記 5Joshua 5

1
ヨルダン川西側のすべてのアモリ人の王たち、および海岸沿いのすべてのカナン人の王たちは、イスラエルの人々が渡る間、主が彼らの前でヨルダン川の水を干上がらせられたことを聞き、気力を失い、イスラエルの人々への恐れのために息もできないほどであった。
When all the Amorite kings on the west side of the Jordan and all the Canaanite kings along the seacoast heard how the Lord had dried up the water of the Jordan before the Israelites while they crossed, they lost their courage and could not even breathe for fear of the Israelites.

At that time the Lord told Joshua, “Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites once again.”

そこでヨシュアは火打石の小刀を作り、包皮の丘でイスラエルの人々に割礼を施した。
So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at the Hill of the Foreskins.
1
4
ヨシュアが割礼を施さなければならなかった理由は次のとおりである。エジプトを出たとき戦うことのできる年齢であったすべての男子は、エジプトを出た後の荒野の旅の途上で死んだからである。
This is why Joshua had to circumcise them: All the men old enough to fight when they left Egypt died on the journey through the wilderness after they left Egypt.
1
5
出て来た民はみな割礼を受けていたが、エジプトを出た後の荒野の旅の途上で生まれた男子は、だれも割礼を受けていなかった。
Now all the men who left were circumcised, but all the sons born on the journey through the wilderness after they left Egypt were uncircumcised.
1 2 3
6
実にイスラエルの人々は、エジプトを出たとき戦うことのできる年齢であった男子のうち、主に従わなかった者たちが死に絶えるまで四十年間荒野を旅した。主は先祖たちに誓われた土地、乳と蜜の流れる豊かな土地を彼らには見せないと誓われたからである。
Indeed, for 40 years the Israelites traveled through the wilderness until all the men old enough to fight when they left Egypt, the ones who had disobeyed the Lord, died off. For the Lord had sworn a solemn oath to them that he would not let them see the land he had sworn by oath to their ancestors to give them, a land rich in milk and honey.
1 2 3
7
主は彼らに代えてその息子たちを立てられ、ヨシュアは彼らに割礼を施した。彼らは道中で割礼を受けておらず、無割礼のままであったからである。
He replaced them with their sons, whom Joshua circumcised. They were uncircumcised; their fathers had not circumcised them along the way.
1
8
民全体が割礼を受け終えると、彼らは傷が癒えるまで宿営のその場にとどまった。
When all the men had been circumcised, they stayed there in the camp until they had healed.
1
9
主はヨシュアに言われた。「今日、わたしはエジプトのはずかしめをあなたがたから転がり去らせた。」それゆえ、その場所の名は今日に至るまでギルガルと呼ばれている。
The Lord said to Joshua, “Today I have taken away the disgrace of Egypt from you.” So that place is called Gilgal even to this day.

So the Israelites camped in Gilgal and celebrated the Passover in the evening of the fourteenth day of the month in the rift valley plains of Jericho.

They ate some of the produce of the land the day after the Passover, including unleavened bread and roasted grain.

The manna stopped appearing the day they ate some of the produce of the land; the Israelites never ate manna again. They ate from the produce of the land of Canaan that year.

When Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him holding a drawn sword. Joshua approached him and asked him, “Are you on our side or allied with our enemies?”

He answered, “Truly I am the commander of the Lord’s army. Now I have arrived!” Joshua bowed down with his face to the ground and asked, “What does my master want to say to his servant?”

The commander of the Lord’s army answered Joshua, “Remove your sandals from your feet because the place where you stand is holy.” Joshua did so.

1:1

[写本注] Another textual tradition has, “while we crossed.”

[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「彼らの心は溶け去り、イスラエルの子らのゆえに彼らの中にはもはや息がなくなった」。
[翻訳注] Heb “their heart[s] melted and there was no longer in them breathe because of the sons of Israel.”
2:1
[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「再びイスラエルの子らに二度目の割礼を施せ」。ヘブル語の「シューヴ（立ち返る、再び）」という語は、動作の反復を示す副詞的な意味で用いられている。
[翻訳注] Heb “return, circumcise the sons of Israel a second time.” The Hebrew term שׁוּブ (shuv, “return”) is used here in an adverbial sense to indicate the repetition of an action.
3:1
[翻訳注] または「ギベアテ・ハアラロテ」。この地名は「包皮の丘」を意味する。多くの現代訳ではヘブル語の地名をそのまま表記しているが、地名の意味を説明する注記が含まれることも多い。[解説] この場所に付けられた「包皮の丘」という名は、この重要な出来事を思い起こさせる明白な記念であった。
[翻訳注] Or “Gibeath Haaraloth.” This name means “Hill of the Foreskins.” Many modern translations simply give the Hebrew name, although an explanatory note giving the meaning of the name is often included.[解説] The name given to the place, Hill of the Foreskins was an obvious reminder of this important event.

[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「エジプトを出たすべての民、すなわち男子、すべての戦士たちは、エジプトを出た後、荒野の途上で死んだ」。

[翻訳注] Heb “flowing with.”[解説] The word picture a land rich in milk and honey depicts the land as containing many grazing areas (which would produce milk) and flowering plants (which would support the bees that produced honey).

9:2
[解説] 「エジプトのはずかしめ」を彼らの無割礼の状態への言及と解釈することもできるが（創世記34:14参照）、エジプトを出た世代は割礼を受けていた（5節参照）。これは彼らがエジプトの奴隷状態で経験した恥辱を指している可能性が高い。この新しい世代が約束の地に到達し、割礼の儀式を受けることによって主への契約の誓約を新たにしたとき、十の災いや葦の海の横断から始まった主による奴隷状態からの民の救出劇は頂点に達した。T・C・バトラー『ヨシュア記（WBC）』59頁参照。
[解説] One might take the disgrace of Egypt as a reference to their uncircumcised condition (see Gen 34:14), but the generation that left Egypt was circumcised (see v. 5). It more likely refers to the disgrace they experienced in Egyptian slavery. When this new generation reached the promised land and renewed their covenantal commitment to the Lord by submitting to the rite of circumcision, the Lord’s deliverance of his people from slavery, which had begun with the plagues and the crossing of the Red Sea, reached its climax. See T. C. Butler, Joshua (WBC), 59.

[解説] The name Gilgal sounds like the Hebrew verb “roll away” (גַּלַל, galal).

[地図] For location see Map5-B2; Map6-E1; Map7-E1; Map8-E3; Map10-A2; Map11-A1.
13:3
[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「彼は目を上げて見た。見よ、一人の人が抜き身の剣を手にして彼の前に立っていた」。動詞「ヒンネー（見よ、הִנֵּה）」は、読者がヨシュアの視点を通して情景を見るように促している。見知らぬ者を「人」と呼ぶことで、著者はヨシュアの認識を反映している。本文ではまもなく彼の真の正体が明らかにされる（14-15節）。
[翻訳注] Heb “he lifted up his eyes and looked. And look, a man was standing in front of him, and his sword was drawn in his hand.” The verb הִנֵּה (hinneh, “look”) invites the reader to view the scene through Joshua’s eyes. By calling the stranger “a man,” the author reflects Joshua’s perspective. The text shortly reveals his true identity (vv. 14-15).
13:4
[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「あなたは私たちの味方か、それとも敵の味方か」。
[翻訳注] Heb “Are you for us or for our enemies?”
14:1
[写本注] ヘブル語原文は「彼は『いや』と言った」。異読として「ロー（לוֹ、『彼に［言った］』；NEB参照）」がある。この読みは七十人訳やシリア語訳だけでなく、多くのヘブル語写本によっても支持されている。マソラ本文の伝統的な読み（ロー、לֹא、『いいえ、どちらでもない』）は、聴覚的な混同（二つの異読はヘブル語で同音）によるものである可能性が高い。多くの現代訳（NIV、NRSV参照）がこれに従っているが、主の軍の将軍が自らを中立と宣言することは考えにくいため、この読みには問題がある。
[写本注] Heb “He said, “Neither.” An alternative reading is לוֹ (lo, “[He said] to him”; cf. NEB). This reading is supported by many Hebrew mss, as well as the LXX and Syriac versions. The traditional reading of the MT (לֹא, lo’, “no, neither”) is probably the product of aural confusion (the two variant readings sound the same in Hebrew). Although followed by a number of modern translations (cf. NIV, NRSV), this reading is problematic, for the commander of the Lord’s army would hardly have declared himself neutral.
14:2
[解説] 主の天の軍勢には、地上の軍隊と同様に軍勢を率いる指揮官がいる。人間の領域における「軍の将軍（サル・ツァヴァー、שַׂר־צְבָא）」という表現については、創世記21:22、32、26:26、士師記4:2、7、サムエル記上12:9など多数の箇所を参照。
[解説] The Lord’s heavenly army, like an earthly army, has a commander who leads the troops. For the phrase שַׂר־צְבָא (sar-tsÿva’, “army commander”) in the human sphere, see among many other references Gen 21:22, 32; 26:26; Judg 4:2, 7; 1 Sam 12:9.
14:3
[解説] 指揮官の出現はヨシュアを励ますためのものであったと考えられる。主の見えない軍勢が勝利を確実なものにすると知ったヨシュアは、確信を持ってイスラエルを率いて戦いに臨むことができた。
[解説] The commander’s appearance seems to be for Joshua’s encouragement. Joshua could now lead Israel into battle knowing that the Lord’s invisible army would ensure victory.
14:4
[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「ヨシュアは顔を地に伏せてひれ伏した」。
[翻訳注] Heb “Joshua fell on his face to the ground and bowed down.”
