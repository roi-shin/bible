[翻訳注] 「時が来た」という句は原語では節の冒頭にのみ現れるが、強調と対比のために繰り返して訳出されている。ギリシャ語では一つの定動詞（「来た」）に複合主語（「御怒り」「時」）が続き、その後に三つの不定詞節（「裁かれる」「与えられる」「滅ぼす」）が置かれている。
[翻訳注] The words “the time has come” do not occur except at the beginning of the verse; the phrase has been repeated for emphasis and contrast. The Greek has one finite verb (“has come”) with a compound subject (“your wrath,” “the time”), followed by three infinitive clauses (“to be judged,” “to give,” “to destroy”). The rhetorical power of the repetition of the finite verb in English thus emulates the rhetorical power of its lone instance in Greek.

[翻訳注] Or “who deprave.” There is a possible wordplay here on two meanings for διαφθείρω (diafqeirw), with the first meaning “destroy” and the second meaning either “to ruin” or “to make morally corrupt.” See L&N 20.40.
