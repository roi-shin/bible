1:1
[翻訳注] 原文では「さて、〜となった」（エゲネト）という表現があるが、ルカ文書に多用されるこの表現は現代の日本語や英語では冗長であるため訳出していない。ここでは、新しいトピックへの移行を示すために「さて」と訳している。
[翻訳注] Grk “Now it happened that one.” The introductory phrase ἐγένετο (egeneto, “it happened that”), common in Luke (69 times) and Acts (54 times), is redundant in contemporary English and has not been translated. Here καί (kai) has been translated as “now” to indicate the transition to a new topic.

4:1
[解説] ヨハネはイエスと同様に、公式のラビ階層の出身ではなかった。そのため、「ヨハネのバプテスマは、天からのものだったか、それとも人からのものだったか」という質問は、洗礼者ヨハネとイエスの間に類似性を見出させるものである（ルカ3:1-20; 7:24-27参照）。「ヨハネのバプテスマ」という表現は、ヨハネが行っていたバプテスマを指している。
[解説] John, like Jesus, was not a part of the official rabbinic order. So the question “John’s baptism – was it from heaven or from men?” draws an analogy between John the Baptist and Jesus. See Luke 3:1-20; 7:24-27. The phrase John’s baptism refers to the baptism practiced by John.
4:2
[翻訳注] ギリシア語の複数形アンスローポン（「人々」）は、ここでも（6節でも）包括的な意味で使用されており、男女双方を指している。 [解説] この質問は、ヨハネの宣教が神に由来するものか、あるいは人間に由来するものかという点を問うものである。
[翻訳注] The plural Greek term ἀνθρώπων (anqrwpwn) is used here (and in v. 6) in a generic sense, referring to both men and women (cf. NAB, NRSV, “of human origin”; TEV, “from human beings”; NLT, “merely human”).[解説] The question is whether John’s ministry was of divine or human origin.

[翻訳注] Here καί (kai) has been translated as “so” to indicate the implied result of the dilemma Jesus’ opponents faced.
7:2
[解説] 宗教指導者たちの邪悪な意図をこれほどまでに完全に暴いた質問はほかにほとんどない。イエスの質問は宗教指導者たちの動機を暴き、彼らが偽善者であるという本性を露わにした。彼らが二つの選択肢しか挙げず、そのどちらも選ばなかったとき、彼らは自分自身を有罪としたのである。ルカ20:1-8の要点は、イエスが彼らの質問に対して何と答えようとも、彼らはそれを信じることはなく、最終的にはイエスを陥れるためにそれを用いるということであった。
[解説] Very few questions could have so completely revealed the wicked intentions of the religious leaders. Jesus’ question revealed the motivation of the religious leaders and exposed them for what they really were – hypocrites. They indicted themselves when they cited only two options and chose neither of them. The point of Luke 20:1-8 is that no matter what Jesus said in response to their question they were not going to believe it and would in the end use it against him.

[翻訳注] パレスチナで流通していたすべてのコインがカエサルの肖像を持っていたわけではないため、ここでは特定の硬貨の名（デナリオン）を維持している。 [解説] デナリオンは労働者の約1日分の賃金に相当する銀貨であった。
[翻訳注] Here the specific name of the coin was retained in the translation, because not all coins in circulation in Palestine at the time carried the image of Caesar. In other places δηνάριον (dhnarion) has been translated simply as “silver coin” with an explanatory note.[解説] A denarius was a silver coin worth approximately one day’s wage for a laborer. The fact that the leaders had such a coin showed that they already operated in the economic world of Rome. The denarius would have had a picture of Tiberius Caesar, the Roman emperor, on it.

This same Greek word is used in Gen 1:26 (LXX) to state that humanity is made in the “image” of God. Jesus is making a subtle yet powerful contrast: Caesar’s image is on the denarius, so he can lay claim to money through taxation, but God’s image is on humanity, so he can lay claim to each individual life.

[解説] 申命記25:5からの引用。これはレビラト婚（収穫婚）と呼ばれる慣習である。

This practice is called levirate marriage (see also Ruth 4:1-12; Mishnah, m. Yevamot; Josephus, Ant. 4.8.23 [4.254-256]). The levirate law is described in Deut 25:5-10. The brother of a man who died without a son had an obligation to marry his brother’s widow. This served several purposes: It provided for the widow in a society where a widow with no children to care for her would be reduced to begging, and it preserved the name of the deceased, who would be regarded as the legal father of the first son produced from that marriage.

[解説] The Lord said to my Lord. With David being the speaker, this indicates his respect for his descendant (referred to as my Lord). In Jerusalem, the king’s palace was located to the right of the temple to indicate this kind of relationship. Jesus was pressing the language here to get his opponents to reflect on how great Messiah is.
