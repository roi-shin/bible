In the second year of the reign of Israel’s King Joash son of Joahaz, Joash’s son Amaziah became king over Judah.

[翻訳注] ヘブル語「ヤロボアムの残りの出来事、および彼がしたすべてのこと、彼の強さ、［そして］彼がどのように戦い、どのようにダマスコとハマテをイスラエルにおけるユダに回復させたかについては、イスラエルの王たちの時代の出来事の巻物に記されていないだろうか」。「ユダに」という語句はおそらく原初のものではなく、ユダで統治していたダビデとソロモンのかつての業績とヤロボアムの征服を結びつけようとしたユダの写字生による追加である可能性がある。シリア語ペシタ訳には単に「イスラエルに」とある。M・コーガンおよびH・タドモール（II Kings [AB], 162）はこの説を提示しているが、それが「き極めて推測的」であることは認めている。
[翻訳注] Heb “As for the rest of the events of Jeroboam, and all which he did and his strength, [and] how he fought and how he restored Damascus and Hamath to Judah in Israel, are they not written on the scroll of the events of the days of the kings of Israel?” The phrase “to Judah” is probably not original; it may be a scribal addition by a Judahite scribe who was trying to link Jeroboam’s conquests with the earlier achievements of David and Solomon, who ruled in Judah. The Syriac Peshitta has simply “to Israel.” M. Cogan and H. Tadmor (II Kings [AB], 162) offer this proposal, but acknowledge that it is “highly speculative.”

[翻訳注] The MT has simply “with the kings of Israel,” which appears to stand in apposition to the immediately preceding “with his fathers.” But it is likely that the words “and he was buried in Samaria” have been accidentally omitted from the text. See 13:13 and 14:16.
