Later King Nahash of the Ammonites died and his son succeeded him.

David said, “I will express my loyalty to Hanun son of Nahash, for his father was loyal to me.” So David sent messengers to express his sympathy over his father’s death. When David’s servants entered Ammonite territory to visit Hanun and express the king’s sympathy,

People came and told David what had happened to the men, so he sent messengers to meet them, for the men were thoroughly humiliated. The king said, “Stay in Jericho until your beards grow again; then you may come back.”

When the Ammonites realized that David was disgusted with them, Hanun and the Ammonites sent 1,000 talents of silver to hire chariots and charioteers from Aram Naharaim, Aram Maacah, and Zobah.

6:2
ヘブライ語のキカル（kikar、「円」）という言葉は、一般に丸いものを指す。金属に関して用いられる場合、その金属で作られた円盤状の分銅、あるいは拡張されて重量の標準単位を指す。古いバビロニアの基準によれば、「タラント」は約130ポンド（58.9キログラム）であったが、後に108.3ポンド（49.1キログラム）に引き下げられた。近年の研究では、「軽量」標準タラントは約67.3ポンド（30.6キログラム）であったことが示唆されている。これを計算の基準とすると、アモン人は約33.7トン（30,600キログラム）の銀で戦車と戦車兵を雇ったことになる。
[翻訳注] The Hebrew word כִּכַּר (kikar, “circle”) refers generally to something that is round. When used of metals it can refer to a disk-shaped weight made of the metal or, by extension, to a standard unit of weight. According to the older (Babylonian) standard the “talent” weighed 130 lbs. (58.9 kg), but later this was lowered to 108.3 lbs. (49.1 kg). More recent research suggests the “light” standard talent was 67.3 lbs. (30.6 kg). Using this as the standard for calculation, the Ammonites hired chariots and charioteers for about 33.7 tons (30,600 kg) of silver.
