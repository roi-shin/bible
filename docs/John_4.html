On the other hand, this Gospel speaks of Jesus as Lord in the evangelist’s narrative descriptions elsewhere only in 11:2; 20:18, 20; 21:12; and probably 6:23, preferring ᾿Ιησοῦς most of the time. This fact could be used to argue that scribes, acquainted with John’s style, changed κύριος to ᾿Ιησοῦς. But the immediate context generally is weighed more heavily than an author’s style. It is possible that neither word was in the original text and scribes supplied what they thought most appropriate (see TCGNT 176). But without ms evidence to this effect coupled with the harder reading ᾿Ιησοῦς, this conjecture must remain doubtful. All in all, it is best to regard ᾿Ιησοῦς as the original reading here.

4:2
[解説] サマリア。サマリア人は主に二つの集団の子孫であった。(1) 紀元前722年の北王国の滅亡後に捕囚されず残った現地のイスラエル人の残りの者、(2) アッシリアに忠誠を尽くす住民を定住させるために、アッシリアの征服者によってバビロニアやメディアから移住させられた異邦人の入植者である。サマリア人がエルサレムでの礼拝を拒否したため、サマリア人とユダヤ人の間には神学的な対立が存在した。バビロン捕囚後、サマリア人はユダヤ人によるエルサレムの再建を妨害し、紀元前2世紀にはユダヤ人に対するシリアの戦争を支援した。紀元前128年、ユダヤの大祭司は報復としてゲリジム山にあったサマリアの神殿を焼き払った。
[解説] Samaria. The Samaritans were descendants of 2 groups: (1) The remnant of native Israelites who were not deported after the fall of the Northern Kingdom in 722 b.c.; (2) Foreign colonists brought in from Babylonia and Media by the Assyrian conquerors to settle the land with inhabitants who would be loyal to Assyria. There was theological opposition between the Samaritans and the Jews because the former refused to worship in Jerusalem. After the exile the Samaritans put obstacles in the way of the Jewish restoration of Jerusalem, and in the 2nd century b.c. the Samaritans helped the Syrians in their wars against the Jews. In 128 b.c. the Jewish high priest retaliated and burned the Samaritan temple on Mount Gerizim.

[翻訳注] The substantival ἵνα (Jina) clause has been translated as an English infinitive clause.[解説] No one brought him anything to eat, did they? In the discussion with the disciples which took place while the woman had gone into the city, note again the misunderstanding: The disciples thought Jesus referred to physical food, while he was really speaking figuratively and spiritually again. Thus Jesus was forced to explain what he meant, and the explanation that his food was his mission, to do the will of God and accomplish his work, leads naturally into the metaphor of the harvest. The fruit of his mission was represented by the Samaritans who were coming to him.

[解説] ヨハネ4:44-45。45節の最後の部分は著者による挿入注である。これらの節における主な難点は、44節でイエスが述べられたことわざと、45節におけるガリラヤ人たちの歓迎との間の矛盾にある。オリゲネスは「自分の故郷」を（イエスが去ったばかりの）ユダヤであってガリラヤではないと解釈することでこの問題を解決した。しかしこれは、イエスをガリラヤと結びつけることに腐心し（1:46参照）、ユダヤでの誕生に言及すらしないヨハネ福音書の趣旨に反する。R・E・ブラウンは現代のアプローチを代表している。彼は44節を、ガリラヤでのイエスの不満足な受け入れを強調しようとした後代の編者による付加と見なしている。しかし、「敬う」という言葉をだれかに真の価値を認めるという意味で理解するならば、どちらの便法も不要である。ガリラヤ人たちは確かにイエスを歓迎したが、彼らの歓迎は祭りでイエスがなさったことを見たことに基づく表面的な反応にすぎないことが明らかになる。彼らが見たしるしによって、ニコデモがしるしに基づいて信じた以上にイエスに信仰を置くに至ったという兆候はない。奇跡への熱狂に基づく表面的な歓迎は、真の敬意ではまったくないのである。
[解説] John 4:44-45. The last part of v. 45 is a parenthetical note by the author. The major problem in these verses concerns the contradiction between the proverb stated by Jesus in v. 44 and the reception of the Galileans in v. 45. Origen solved the problem by referring his own country to Judea (which Jesus had just left) and not Galilee. But this runs counter to the thrust of John’s Gospel, which takes pains to identify Jesus with Galilee (cf. 1:46) and does not even mention his Judean birth. R. E. Brown typifies the contemporary approach: He regards v. 44 as an addition by a later redactor who wanted to emphasize Jesus’ unsatisfactory reception in Galilee. Neither expedient is necessary, though, if honor is understood in its sense of attributing true worth to someone. The Galileans did welcome him, but their welcome was to prove a superficial response based on what they had seen him do at the feast. There is no indication that the signs they saw brought them to place their faith in Jesus any more than Nicodemus did on the basis of the signs. But a superficial welcome based on enthusiasm for miracles is no real honor at all.
