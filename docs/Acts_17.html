[解説] Thessalonica (modern Salonica) was a city in Macedonia about 33 mi (53 km) west of Apollonia. It was the capital of Macedonia. The road they traveled over was called the Via Egnatia. It is likely they rode horses, given their condition in Philippi. The implication of v. 1 is that the two previously mentioned cities lacked a synagogue.[地図] For location see JP1-C1; JP2-C1; JP3-C1; JP4-C1.

2:2
[翻訳注] 「διελέξατο」（dielexato、「διαλέγομαι」dialegomaiより）という語は「論じた」「議論した」としばしば訳されますが、この意味は問答法を含む哲学的論争に使われた古典ギリシャ語の意味に由来します。しかし、使徒の働き17章2節においてそのような討論が行われたとする文脈上の根拠は見当たりません。G・シュレンク（TDNT 2:94-95）が指摘するように「ここで問題となっているのは、会堂の資格ある会員なら誰でも行うような講話・説教である」ということです。新約聖書における他の例としては、マタイ4章23節やマルコ1章21節が挙げられます。
[翻訳注] Although the word διελέξατο (dielexato; from διαλέγομαι, dialegomai) is frequently translated “reasoned,” “disputed,” or “argued,” this sense comes from its classical meaning where it was used of philosophical disputation, including the Socratic method of questions and answers. However, there does not seem to be contextual evidence for this kind of debate in Acts 17:2. As G. Schrenk (TDNT 2:94-95) points out, “What is at issue is the address which any qualified member of a synagogue might give.” Other examples of this may be found in the NT in Matt 4:23 and Mark 1:21.

[翻訳注] The Greek words used here (καὶ ὅτι, kai {oti, “and that”) mark the switch from indirect to direct discourse. Contemporary English requires the use of an introductory verb of speaking or saying to make this transition.
