NET Notes (翻訳者注)

1:1
[解説] 本章の召命の前半部（第3章）において、ヤハウェはご自身の民を救い出すと約束される。モーセの躊躇に対し、神はご臨在が彼と共にあることを保証し、それが使命の成功を確かなものとする。しかし召命の後半部である第4章に入ると、語調は一変する。ここでモーセは、任務の性質に照らして自らの不適格さを訴えるのである。多方面において、これらの節は「だれがこれらの任務に堪えうるのか」という問いに答えている。この段落には三つの基本的な展開がある。最初の9節は、イスラエルが彼を信じない場合に備えて神がモーセにしるしを与えられた経緯を語る（4:1–9）。第2の区分は、モーセの弁舌の問題を神がどのように扱われたかを記す（4:10–12）。そして最後の区分は、雄弁に語ることのできる協力者を神が備えられたことを記録している（4:13–17）。J. E. Hamlin, “The Liberator’s Ordeal: A Study of Exodus 4:1-9,” Rhetorical Criticism [PTMS], 33–42 も参照。
[解説] In chap. , the first part of this extensive call, Yahweh promises to deliver his people. At the hesitancy of Moses, God guarantees his presence will be with him, and that assures the success of the mission. But with chap. , the second half of the call, the tone changes sharply. Now Moses protests his inadequacies in view of the nature of the task. In many ways, these verses address the question, “Who is sufficient for these things?” There are three basic movements in the passage. The first nine verses tell how God gave Moses signs in case Israel did not believe him (4:1-9). The second section records how God dealt with the speech problem of Moses (4:10-12). And finally, the last section records God’s provision of a helper, someone who could talk well (4:13-17). See also J. E. Hamlin, “The Liberator’s Ordeal: A Study of Exodus 4:1-9,” Rhetorical Criticism [PTMS], 33-42.

[解説] これは強力なしるしである。ナイル川はエジプトにおける命の源として知られていたが、今や死の証拠となるからである。このように三つのしるしは類似しており、それぞれが生と死から構成されている。これらは十の災いを通じたエジプトとの対決を明確に予告している。民が信じない可能性に直面しても、神のしもべは神の言葉を伝えるにあたり、神の力の明確な証拠を提示しなければならないという点が明白である。その後の結果は神に委ねられている。
[解説] This is a powerful sign, for the Nile was always known as the source of life in Egypt, but now it will become the evidence of death. So the three signs were alike, each consisting of life and death. They would clearly anticipate the struggle with Egypt through the plagues. The point is clear that in the face of the possibility that people might not believe, the servants of God must offer clear proof of the power of God as they deliver the message of God. The rest is up to God.
10:1
[解説] ここでモーセは別の論点を持ち出す。自らが流暢に語ることができないという問題である（10–17節）。ここでの要点は、神のしもべは創造主である神の器として自らを委ねなければならないということである。神が共におられるならば、どのような性格的特徴を持っていようと、自らがどのような弱点を抱えていると考えていようと（モーセは実際には見事に語っている）、何の違いも生じない。主権者なる神が彼らを選ばれたのであれば、神が意図された必要なものはすべて備えられているのである。
[解説] Now Moses took up another line of argumentation, the issue of his inability to speak fluently (vv. 10-17). The point here is that God’s servants must yield themselves as instruments to God, the Creator. It makes no difference what character traits they have or what weaknesses they think they have (Moses manages to speak very well) if God is present. If the sovereign God has chosen them, then they have everything that God intended them to have.
10:2
[翻訳注] ビー（בִּי, bi）という語は懇願の小辞であり、発言の許可を求めるもので、常に「わが主」が後に続く。しばしば「どうか」「ああ」と訳され、「請願、不服申し立て、弁明において用いられる」（W. H. C. Propp, Exodus 1–18 [AB], 213）。
[翻訳注] The word בִּי (bi) is a particle of entreaty; it seeks permission to speak and is always followed by “my lord” or “my Lord.” Often rendered “please,” it is “employed in petitions, complaints and excuses” (W. H. C. Propp, –18 [AB], 213).
10:3
[翻訳注] モーセの呼びかけにおけるアドナイ（אֲדֹנָי, ’adonay）という呼称は、「主、主人、閣下」といった敬意と服従を表す言葉であるが、神聖四文字（テトラグラマトン）を表す場合の母音記号が付されている。B. ヤコブは、モーセがヤハウェに直接語りかけたのはこれが初めてであったため、躊躇しながら語ったと述べている（Exodus, 87）。
[翻訳注] The designation in Moses’ address is אֲדֹנָי (’adonay), a term of respect and deference such as “lord, master, sir” but pointed as it would be when it represents the tetragrammaton. B. Jacob says since this is the first time Moses spoke directly to Yahweh, he did so hesitatingly (Exodus, 87).

[翻訳注] When a noun clause is negated with לֹא (lo’), rather than אֵין (’en), there is a special emphasis, since the force of the negative falls on a specific word (GKC 479 §152.d). The expression “eloquent man” is אִישׁ דְּבָרִים (’ish dÿvarim, “a man of words”). The genitive may indicate a man characterized by words or a man who is able to command or control words. Moses apparently is resigned to the fact that he can do the signs, but he knows the signs have to be explained.

[翻訳注] The two expressions are כְבַד־פֶּה (khÿvad peh, “heavy of mouth”), and then כְבַד לָשׁוֹן (khÿvad lashon, “heavy of tongue”). Both use genitives of specification, the mouth and the tongue being what are heavy – slow. “Mouth” and “tongue” are metonymies of cause. Moses is saying that he has a problem speaking well. Perhaps he had been too long at the other side of the desert, or perhaps he was being a little dishonest. At any rate, he has still not captured the meaning of God’s presence. See among other works, J. H. Tigay, “‘Heavy of Mouth’ and ‘Heavy of Tongue’: On Moses’ Speech Difficulty,” BASOR 231 (1978): 57-67.

The form carries the instructional meaning because it follows the imperative “go.” In fact, there is a sequence at work here: “go…and/that I may teach you.” It is from יָרָה (yara), the same root behind תּוֹרָה (torah, “law”). [解説] The metaphor uses the word “son” in its connotation of a political dependent, as it was used in ancient documents to describe what was intended to be a loyal relationship with well-known privileges and responsibilities, like that between a good father and son. If the people of Israel were God’s “son,” then they should serve him and not Pharaoh. Malachi reminds people that the Law said “a son honors his father,” and so God asked, “If I am a father, where is my honor?” (Mal 1:6).

In both cases there is a brush with death that could not be forgotten. See also, W. Dumbrell, “Exodus 4:24-25: A Textual Re-examination,” HTR 65 (1972): 285-90; T. C. The implication is certainly that they prayed, or praised, and performed some other aspect of worship, but the text may just be describing it from their posture of worship. With this response, all the fears of Moses are swept aside – they believed and they were thankful to God.
