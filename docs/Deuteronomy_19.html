[翻訳注] Heb “innocent blood must not be shed.” The Hebrew phrase דָּם נָקִי (dam naqiy) means the blood of a person to whom no culpability or responsibility adheres because what he did was without malice aforethought (HALOT 224 s.v דָּם 4.b).

[翻訳注] The גֹאֵל הַדָּם (go’el haddam, “avenger of blood”) would ordinarily be a member of the victim’s family who, after due process of law, was invited to initiate the process of execution (cf. Num 35:16-28). See R. Hubbard, NIDOTTE 1:789-94.
13:1
[解説] 罪のない者の血を除き去る。イスラエルの共同体生活の連帯的性格により、復讐が果たされるまでは、復讐されていない殺人の罪責を共同体全体が共有していた。これによってのみ、霊的かつ道徳的な均衡が回復された（民数記 35:33）。
[解説] Purge out the blood of the innocent. Because of the corporate nature of Israel’s community life, the whole community shared in the guilt of unavenged murder unless and until vengeance occurred. Only this would restore spiritual and moral equilibrium (Num 35:33).

19:1
[翻訳注] ヘブル語原文は「焼き尽くす」（בִּעַרְתָּ, bi’arta）。未解決の罪は共同体全体を蝕むため、悔い改めない加害者を排除することによって除去されなければならない（申命記 13:6、17:7, 12、21:21、22:21-22, 24、24:7参照）。
[翻訳注] Heb “you will burn out” (בִּעַרְתָּ, bi’arta). Like a cancer, unavenged sin would infect the whole community. It must, therefore, be excised by the purging out of its perpetrators who, presumably, remained unrepentant (cf. Deut 13:6; 17:7, 12; 21:21; 22:21-22, 24; 24:7).
21:1
[解説] This kind of justice is commonly called lex talionis or “measure for measure” (cf. Exod 21:23-25; Lev 24:19-20). It is likely that it is the principle that is important and not always a strict application. That is, the punishment should fit the crime and it may do so by the payment of fines or other suitable and equitable compensation (cf. Exod 22:21; Num 35:31). See T. S. Frymer-Kensky, “Tit for Tat: The Principle of Equal Retribution in Near Eastern and Biblical Law,” BA 43 (1980): 230-34.
